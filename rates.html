Where these rates sit in the market

Competitiveness over the year: the same money measured against five official reference points, each read on or after 2026-10-19.

FDIC national rates and the Federal Reserve's target range are government-published; nothing here comes from a rate aggregator.
Reference pointRateAs ofWhat it means
FDIC national rate — savings0.38%2026-10-19The average insured savings account in the country. The high-yield accounts here pay roughly 8 to 11 times this; the branch-bank tiers pay a fraction of it. fdic.gov
FDIC national rate cap — savings4.63%2026-10-19A regulatory ceiling that applies only to banks that are less than well capitalized, not a market rate. No bank on this page is anywhere near it; it is context, not a target. fdic.gov
U.S. Treasury — 6-month bill4.33%2026-10-19A 6-month government bill yield as published by the FDIC. Slightly above the best no-conditions savings rate, but the money is locked for six months. fdic.gov
U.S. Treasury — 12-month bill4.54%2026-10-19The one-year government yield. Treasury interest is exempt from state and local income tax; savings interest is not. fdic.gov
Federal funds target range3.75%–4.00%2026-09-17Set by the FOMC; raised 25 bp at the 2026-09-17 meeting after three cuts in 2025. Deposit rates tend to follow this floor with a lag. federalreserve.gov
A savings APY is variable, so a 4.20% account can fall below a 4.54% one-year Treasury before the year is out. The Treasury locks the yield; the savings account keeps the money liquid. That is the actual trade-off, not the headline number.
