The whole decision in one paragraph

verified
Same $250,000. Same year. Same federal insurance. $10,500 of interest at 4.20% (EverBank), $950 at the FDIC national average of 0.38%, $125 at U.S. Bank's standard 0.05%, $75 at PNC's standard 0.03%, $25 at Wells Fargo's published 0.01%. Add the six-month introductory rates and the best year one on $250,000 is $10,618 at Axos ONE (4.25% blended) — but only if you run the $1,500-a-month direct deposit and $1,500 average balance through its checking account, and only while the balance sits at or above $250,000. The gap between the best verified rate and the worst published branch-bank rate is $10,475 a year on the same insured deposit — about 425× the interest. FDIC insurance does not depend on which of these banks you pick, so accepting a branch-bank rate is a choice, not a requirement.

Read before acting
  • Every rate here is variable and can change before or after the account is opened. The projections hold the rate constant only to make the comparison possible. Introductory rates expire — the six-month boosts are shown as separate “derived” rows.
  • The projections are arithmetic on published APYs, not bank quotes. Interest is taxable and is reported on Form 1099-INT.
  • Branch-bank rate sheets are ZIP-gated. U.S. Bank was read at ZIP 55402, PNC at ZIP 15222 and Wells Fargo at CA 94105 — flagged.
  • FDIC coverage is $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Splitting cash across ownership categories or banks — not picking a famous brand — is what raises coverage.
