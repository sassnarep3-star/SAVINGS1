Insurance and calculation method

FDIC coverage and how to read the tables

A coverage limit is a legal insurance cap, not an investment target. Leave headroom for interest growth and other deposits at the same bank.

What the $250,000 limit means

FDIC coverage is generally $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, per account ownership category. Deposits in the same category at one bank are aggregated, including principal and accrued interest. Actual coverage depends on ownership/titling and all deposits held at that bank.

This is not a per-account limit. Multiple single-owner savings accounts at the same bank do not each get their own $250,000 coverage. Separate FDIC-insured banks generally have separate limits; different ownership categories may also be separately insured when FDIC requirements are met.

Deposit headroom example

With an unchanged 4.25% APY, $235,000 would earn about $9,988 in one year and end near $244,988, before tax and assuming no other deposits at the same bank in that ownership category. At $240,000, the estimated balance becomes about $250,200 and exceeds the standard cap.

A $250,000 deposit has no headroom for accrued interest. Leaving a cushion does not guarantee coverage if rates rise, additional money is deposited, or other same-bank balances exist. Use the FDIC's official estimator and check all ownership categories.

Return methodology

  • As-of/opening date: 2026-10-22; the one-year scenario ends 2027-10-22. Three- and five-year scenario endpoints are October 22, 2029 and October 22, 2031.
  • One-year calculation: opening principal × applicable current APY, rounded to the nearest dollar. APY is already an annual yield.
  • Longer calculation: for products explicitly marked eligible, principal × ((1 + APY)years − 1), rounded to the nearest dollar, assuming the same variable APY remains unchanged and interest remains deposited. These are hypothetical, not forecasts.
  • Flows and exclusions: one lump-sum deposit; no additions or withdrawals; fees, taxes and cash bonuses are excluded. Eligible bonus cards list their separate qualification terms.
  • Conditional rates: Wells Fargo uses 0.01% below its $25,000 qualifying minimum and 3.25% only at/above it, for the one-year scenario. Citi's 0.98% New York blended APY is used only for qualifying $25,000+ funds and the account-opening deadline of October 22, 2026; it is not projected beyond one year.
  • Unsupported values: no value is inferred from a nonofficial source. Chase, Bank of America, and U.S. Bank New York APYs are blank; Capital One's numeric comparison-page APY is shown only as a flagged provisional value.
  • Longer-horizon exclusions: promotional rates, location-unconfirmed rates, and official-source conflicts are not extended into future years. Barclays Tiered Savings is not projected past one year because its rate tier changes at $250,000.

Check each official source and the application disclosures on the actual opening date. Rates, fees, eligibility, and promotion terms may change.

Insurance and yield are different: FDIC insurance protects eligible deposits if an insured bank fails, subject to coverage limits and rules. It does not protect against an account's variable APY falling, inflation, taxes, or loss of coverage caused by exceeding the limit. Barclays Tiered Savings and Barclays AARP Select Savings are at the same FDIC-insured bank and aggregate for the same depositor and ownership category.
