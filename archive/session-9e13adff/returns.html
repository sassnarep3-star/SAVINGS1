FDIC LIMIT CAVEAT

$250,000 is a total-balance cap

FDIC coverage includes principal and accrued interest, and aggregates your same-category deposits at the same bank. At a constant 3.25% APY, depositing $250,000 would grow to $258,125 after one year—$8,125 above the standard coverage limit, before considering any other deposits at Wells Fargo.

BOTTOM LINE AT $100,000

Potential difference in year one

At unchanged published rates, Wells Fargo would earn $50 more than Capital One over the first year on $100,000. That narrow edge comes with the $25,000 qualifying-balance and new-customer rules. Capital One requires no minimum and has no monthly fee.

By contrast, the verified NY Bank of America rate at 0.04% would earn $40 on $100,000 in one year. The APY spread is large, but the return calculation does not account for tax or future rate changes.

FDIC HEADROOM

Don't deposit the ceiling blindly

For a single-owner account, all same-category deposits at a given bank count together—including checking, savings and CDs—and interest adds to the insured balance. One bank's separate branches do not create separate limits. Other ownership categories can have separate treatment, but requirements apply.
