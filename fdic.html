FDIC insurance, by certificate number

What the $250,000 limit actually covers, how ownership categories interact, and which banks offer expanded programme coverage.

FDIC coverage is $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category — not $250,000 per account. All deposits in the same category at the same bank are added together. Source: FDIC, Understanding Deposit Insurance.

A joint account doubles the limit only because two depositors each have a $250,000 share; it does not double the per-person limit. Different ownership categories at the same bank can also extend coverage.

Ownership categories the FDIC recognises

  • Single Accounts
  • Joint Accounts
  • Certain Retirement Accounts (e.g., IRAs)
  • Trust Accounts
  • Employee Benefit Plan Accounts
  • Corporation / Partnership / Unincorporated Association Accounts
  • Government Accounts

Balances held in the same category are added together. A single account plus a joint account plus an IRA at one bank can therefore be insured above $250,000 — but the categories must genuinely apply. The FDIC's EDIE tool calculates a specific situation: edie.fdic.gov.

If the goal is the maximum insured balance

Option A: one bank per $250,000

Straightforward and fully transparent. At $1,000,000, four banks at $250,000 each are fully covered in a single ownership category. This dashboard's return tables let you pick the best payer at exactly $250,000.

Option B: programme coverage at one bank

Axos (up to $2.5M) and SoFi (up to $3M) place deposits with participating banks through insured deposit programmes. The FDIC insurance is real, but it depends on those partner banks and the programme's terms — it is not extra insurance issued by the host bank. E*TRADE advertises $500,000 without disclosing the conditions; that item is flagged.

Practical warning: $500,000 in a single-owner savings account at one bank leaves $250,000 uninsured, no matter which bank it is or how high its APY is. Above $250,000, the ranking in Returns should be applied per bank, not to the whole balance.
