This is the third dashboard in this repository. The main dashboard ranks 18 products and the cross-check dashboard independently re-verifies 20. This layer does something different: it goes back to the items those two left blank and resolves them from official regional rate-sheet PDFs and fee schedules, then adds the nationally-available accounts neither dataset carried.

The Big Four, resolved from official rate sheets

Savings APYs the published dashboards left blank, recovered from each bank's own dated, market-specific disclosure documents.

Why these were blank. Chase requires a 5-digit ZIP before it will render a rate table. Bank of America's savings page returns literal $XXXX placeholders to an automated reader. Citi injects its APY at runtime. Wells Fargo's product page shows a rate only for whatever market the page defaults to. All four behaviours are real — but three of these banks also publish the same numbers as static, dated, market-specific documents, which is what this page uses.

The gap between the branch banks and the digital banks

Now that the Big Four numbers are readable, the comparison that the earlier dashboards could not make is straightforward. On a $250,000 balance held for one year: Chase Savings at 0.01% pays $25.00; Bank of America Advantage Savings at 0.04% pays $100.00; Truist One Savings at 0.01% pays $25.00. The best flat rate in this layer, Select Savings from Barclays at 4.25%, pays $10,625 — about 106× the Bank of America figure on the same balance, at a bank with the same $250,000 FDIC limit.

What is still unresolved

Citibank's standard savings APY remains blank. Citi's rate value is injected into the page at runtime and Citi publishes no equivalent static regional rate sheet that could be read. Its promotional feature is documented — but Citi's own fact sheet states the promotional rate feature for new Citi Savings accounts is discontinued effective 23 October 2026, one day after this project's reference opening date. Published as flag S02. PNC's High Yield Savings APY is also left blank, because two official PNC URLs for the same page disagree (3.15% versus 3.30%) and neither prints an effective date in the body — flag S03.
