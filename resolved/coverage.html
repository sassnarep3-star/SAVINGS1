Insuring the maximum, precisely

Ownership categories, the $1,250,000 trust ceiling the other dashboards omit, the shared-certificate traps, and a fully-insured $1,000,000 built from verified rates.

The rule, stated exactly. FDIC insurance is $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category — not per account. All deposits you hold in the same category at the same bank are added together, including accrued interest. Source: FDIC — Deposit Insurance At A Glance.

The ownership categories, and what each one is worth

Ownership categoryLimit at ONE bankMaximum insuredWhat it means in practice
Single accounts$250,000 per owner$250,000One person, no beneficiaries. All single accounts you hold at the same bank are added together.
Joint accounts$500,000 per account (2 owners)$500,000Each co-owner is insured to $250,000 for their share. A two-owner joint account is insured to $500,000, and that is separate from either owner's single accounts.
Trust accounts (revocable + irrevocable)up to $1,250,000 per owner$1,250,000Since 1 April 2024 the FDIC combines revocable (POD/ITF) and irrevocable trusts into one category: $250,000 per eligible beneficiary, capped at five beneficiaries — a $1,250,000 maximum per owner, per bank.
Certain retirement accounts$250,000 per owner$250,000IRAs and certain self-directed retirement deposits are a separate ownership category from single accounts at the same bank.

Stacking categories at a single bank

These categories are independent of each other. One depositor, at one FDIC-insured bank, can therefore be covered well beyond $250,000 — provided each category genuinely applies and is correctly titled.

Added at the same bankCoverage addedRunning total insured
Single account$250,000$250,000
+ Joint account with one other person$500,000$750,000
+ Trust account naming 5 beneficiaries$1,250,000$2,000,000
+ IRA / certain retirement deposits$250,000$2,250,000

The FDIC's own calculator, EDIE, prices a specific combination of accounts and titling. Use it before relying on any stacked figure — the categories must genuinely apply, and beneficiaries must be eligible.

Certificate traps that break a max-coverage plan

Several well-known brands are not separate banks. Openbank and Santander share FDIC Cert #29950. CIT Bank and First-Citizens Bank & Trust share Cert #11063. Alto and BMO Bank N.A. share Cert #16571. Barclays Tiered Savings, Barclays Online Savings and Select Savings from Barclays all share Cert #57203. Capital One 360 and the former Discover Bank share Cert #4297 — Discover's own Cert #5649 is inactive. Depositing $250,000 at each brand in a pair produces $250,000 of coverage, not $500,000. Published as flag S05.

Programme coverage is real, but it is not the host bank's insurance

SoFi advertises up to $3,000,000 and Axos up to $2,500,000 of FDIC coverage. In both cases the host bank provides only the standard $250,000; the excess is swept into partner banks, and the coverage depends on those partners and on you not already holding deposits at them. SoFi's own participating-bank list includes Citibank (#7213), Citizens Bank (#57957) and Morgan Stanley Bank (#32992) — so a depositor who already holds $250,000 at any of those banks directly can lose part of the benefit. Check the partner list before relying on a programme figure.
