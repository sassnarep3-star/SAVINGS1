Five findings

1. One rate is a full point above the Fed's ceiling

Axos's 5.00% is 1.00 pp above the top of the 3.75%–4.00% target range. A bank paying materially above the policy rate on a liquid, insured account is buying deposits for a limited period; Axos limits it to six months. Four other headline rates (SoFi 4.40%, Openbank 4.35%, CIT 4.25%, E*TRADE 4.25%) sit 0.25–0.40 pp above the ceiling — also promotional, but by a smaller margin.

2. A 6-month Treasury beats two of the five top promo rates

The 6-month Treasury yield is 4.33%. CIT and E*TRADE pay 4.25%, below it; only Axos (5.00%), SoFi (4.40%) and Openbank (4.35%) beat it. Treasury interest is also exempt from state and local income tax, while savings interest is not. This does not make Treasuries a substitute for an emergency fund — they are not FDIC-insured and are less liquid — but it sets a hard benchmark for what a promotional savings rate is worth.

3. The 12-month Treasury beats every post-intro rate

The 12-month Treasury yield is 4.54%. Every bank's ongoing savings rate is below it: Axos 4.21%, Openbank 4.00%, CIT 3.75%, E*TRADE 3.75%, Synchrony 3.55%, Marcus 3.50%, SoFi 3.30%. Six-month teasers can beat the 12-month Treasury for half a year, but the steady-state savings rate does not.

4. The promo premium ranges from 0 to 1.10 pp

SoFi's headline is 1.10 pp above its own ongoing rate; Axos's is 0.79 pp; CIT and E*TRADE 0.50 pp; Openbank 0.35 pp. EverBank, Synchrony, Marcus, Amex, Capital One and Ally advertise no promotional premium at all, which makes their rates easier to compare and less likely to fall at the six-month mark.

5. “Life span” changes the winner

Over one year the ranking is driven by introductory rates. Over five years it is driven by the ongoing rate — which is why Axos still leads on paper but its lead narrows, and why EverBank (4.20% with no promo and no conditions) moves up relative to six-month teasers. The five-year table on Returns shows both. All of these rates are variable and none is locked.

6. Some branch-bank rates still require local verification

Chase and Bank of America New York APYs remain blank because the official rate tools are ZIP-gated or placeholder-rendered. Wells Fargo Way2Save remains market-specific and blank for New York, while its separate Platinum Savings row includes a conditional 3.25% new-customer rate. Citi's New York promotion is included only for qualifying $25,000+ funds and accounts opened by 2026-10-22; its 0.98% blended APY is not a general ongoing rate. Where fees are visible they can be avoided only under the banks' stated balance/relationship rules.

What would change this analysis

  • A change in the federal funds target range: the next FOMC decision moves every variable savings rate, usually within weeks.
  • Promotional deadlines: Barclays (10/31/2026), CIT (12/15/2026), E*TRADE (1/10/2027).
  • Axos requirement compliance: missing one month drops the ongoing rate from 4.21% to 1.00%.
  • SoFi Plus subscription status: 4.70% on the first $20,000 is a different product tier from the 4.40% headline.
  • Any bank merger or charter change — notably Santander/Webster (Openbank) and Capital One/Discover.
