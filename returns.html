Return on the size of your deposit

Interest earned if the account is opened on 2026-10-22 and the published APY does not change. Sizes run from $10,000 to $1,000,000 so you can see exactly where more money stops being fully insured at one bank.

Formula: ending = principal × (1 + APY/100)years · APY already includes compounding · 20 accounts · 27 projection rows (including 7 promotional blends) · 414 computed cells

Why APY is the right input An advertised APY already accounts for compounding frequency, so growing a balance at the APY for N years gives the balance the bank's own disclosure implies. No bank-specific quirks (daily accrual, monthly crediting) are modelled — they are already inside the APY.

What the promotions are worth inside these numbers

Three derived rows are marked “derived” in the tables above
  • SoFi, year 1: 4.40% for six months then 3.30% blends to 3.849% — $9,621 on $250,000, versus $8,250 at the plain 3.30%.
  • Marcus, year 1 with one referral: 3.75% (Marcus's own illustration) — $9,375 on $250,000.
  • U.S. Bank relationship tier: 3.50% instead of 0.05% — $8,750 versus $125, but it requires a U.S. Bank checking/Safe Debit/Smartly card and $25,000+ in combined qualifying balances.

How every promotional row was computed

Blended year-one APY = sqrt((1 + promotional APY) × (1 + standard APY)) − 1. Both numbers come from the bank's own page.

5 rows apply a six-month promotion and are labelled “derived” in the tables above; two more derived rows use a fixed published tier (U.S. Bank relationship rate, Marcus referral) and need no blending. Everything else is a single published APY.
