FEDERAL DEPOSIT INSURANCE CORPORATION

What the standard $250,000 limit means

  • Standard coverage is up to $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, per ownership category.
  • FDIC insurance covers the balance dollar-for-dollar, including principal and accrued interest through the date of bank failure.
  • All same-category deposits at the same insured bank are added together—savings, checking, CDs, and money market deposit accounts. Separate branches at the same bank do not have separate limits.
  • Different account ownership categories may have separate coverage if the FDIC's requirements for each category are met. Do not assume titling alone creates coverage; verify using FDIC EDIE.
  • FDIC insurance protects against failure of an insured bank; it is not insurance against theft, fraud or loss of investment value.

HOW TO CHECK INSURANCE

Verify the legal bank

A brand name and the insured bank's legal name can differ, and accounts held through a program or intermediary can have extra requirements. Confirm the named institution in FDIC BankFind and use the FDIC's Electronic Deposit Insurance Estimator (EDIE) for deposits already held at the bank.

  1. Find the legal bank name on the product disclosure or statement.
  2. Check the institution using FDIC BankFind ↗.
  3. Add all your same-category deposits and estimated accrued interest for that bank.
  4. Leave headroom because variable interest can move your total balance above the limit.

FDIC coverage does not make a bank savings APY competitive. The best strategy still depends on access, fees, rate eligibility, account ownership and aggregate balances.
